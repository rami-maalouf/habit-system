# morning ignition

the morning half of [[night-shutdown]]. the plan was made last night. the morning's only job is to execute it with zero friction. the design principle, straight from my record: no decisions and no inputs before ignition.

## the principle

ignition is the entire game. "the hardest part is always starting... as soon as i reach that point, i'm off to the races" (apr 11 / apr 21). i do not have a stopping problem once started, i have the opposite. so the morning is engineered around one thing: firing the first 10 minutes before anything else can steal the day.

## the five rules

1. **stage the runway the night before.**
   when i close the day, i leave the desk in first-action state: the one file, tab, or document the first 10 minutes needs stays open, everything else gets closed. environment shapes habits. this is the uber idling outside instead of the two-hour walk.

2. **the calendar block is the alarm.**
   i already live by prompts (the hourly intentionality popup is my longest-surviving system). the morning alarm is not "wake up", it is "block starts", labeled with the first action from last night's sentence.

3. **no reactive inputs before the first 10 minutes.**
   no DMs, email, notifications, stocks, or feeds until the first action has been started. not "no phone all morning" - just: ignition fires first. this is the direct counter to "every morning, i gaslight myself... micro tasks that feel productive but aren't" (jan 5), and it rides my one 3-year survivor habit: no-breakfast deep work mornings.

4. **i only owe the first 10 minutes.**
   not the whole task, not the whole session. ten minutes on the pre-decided first action. this keeps bad days cheap and removes the negotiation that mornings lose.

5. **the intentionality tracker is the referee.**
   the first popup of the day answers one implicit question: did hour one match last night's sentence? no new tooling - the feedback loop already exists.

## what NOT to add to the morning

- no journaling block.
- no reading.
- no meditation (record: 12 attempts, 0 completions).
- above all, no re-planning. the plan was made at night. morning re-planning is the drift vector: "it has never worked for me to plan things in the morning" (mar 15).
